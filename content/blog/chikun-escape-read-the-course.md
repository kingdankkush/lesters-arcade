## Look beyond Chikun

The character is your position marker; the next obstacle is the decision. Move your attention ahead of Chikun and read the shape of the gap you need. That gives you time to choose a jump instead of reacting when an obstacle is already beside you.

Use the on-screen control hints for your device. Begin in Free Mode and practise the same input a few times before changing your approach.

## Think about the landing

Clearing one obstacle is only half of the decision. Look at the space after it as well. A comfortable landing gives you time to read what follows; a last-moment input can leave you reacting to the next obstacle immediately.

When a run ends, identify one specific timing change to try. Earlier is not always better. The useful question is whether the timing gives you a clean path through the obstacle and into the next approach.

## Let coins support the route

Coins provide a satisfying line to follow, but keep obstacle clearance first. Avoid turning a clean run into a rushed correction just to catch a pickup you have already passed.

As speed increases, use the visual rhythm of the course to stay oriented. Keep enough attention ahead of the character to notice a different obstacle silhouette or a tighter sequence.

## Make the game comfortable

Set audio to a level where feedback is useful. If motion or effects distract you, explore the available settings and choose a calmer presentation. The controls and readability matter more than having every effect at its strongest.

On a phone, settle into a comfortable grip before the run starts. Use a short Free attempt to check that your thumb can reach the controls without covering the approach you need to see.

## Keep practice and Ranked distinct

Free Mode needs no wallet. Ranked starts a separate run with its own entry and server verification. The server replays Chikun’s recorded inputs when checking a Ranked result; an impressive-looking animation alone cannot award a score.
