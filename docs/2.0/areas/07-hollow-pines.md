Status: local Pines W2g geometry/nav/native-browser checkpoint passes; owner playtest, full area design and art acceptance remain open. Part of the continuous 20,000 × 14,000 world. See [brief index](INDEX.md) for shared scales, authorisation and gates.

**Story and flow.** A neglected cemetery has overtaken the forest edge. The mood is uneasy, while the routes and enemy silhouettes remain consistently legible.

**Landmark.** One giant dead tree above clustered cemetery stones, with restrained lanterns marking paths.

**Routes in and out.** Darker trail from Hashwood River plus a second forest route to Rugpull Woods. This corrects the handoff sketch’s single connection and satisfies the two-entrance rule. Both connections pass the local greybox nav checks; official-map integration and whole-world owner playtest remain open.

**Objective staging.** Stage a cemetery objective at a recognisable maintenance or burial structure with a clear approach. Fog and lanterns reinforce the destination without introducing a gameplay visibility radius change.

**Combat and height.** Alternate a cemetery clearing with tighter tree/stones lanes. Keep two exits in fight courts; roots/solid stone walls and low cemetery boundaries provide cover heights. A low bank offers a legal height option in the later traversal slice.

**Secret.** An unusual worn path and a broken boundary lead to the proposed higher-value secret nook. Reward/balance is not fixed here. No darkness-only clue, hidden wall or irreversible exit.

**Art sub-palette.** Blue-grey #4C5664, violet-grey #605D70, ash #8B8D88; board L16/L17.

**Greybox acceptance focus.** Mood must survive reduced motion and low quality without hiding bodies or danger cues. Limited gameplay light radius is an independent unapproved rules decision. Confirm both entrances and the secret with directed nav reachability.

## W2g local layout checkpoint

A walled cemetery clearing sits between two unequal forest masses. The public
walk crosses its broad west/east gates and bends past the northern stones. An
outside southern service path connects those same regional entrances without
crossing the cemetery. The southern boundary is broken into a short central
section, so the court has breathing room and believable maintenance access.

The dead tree stands beyond a break in the north boundary. Its solid faceted
root base is a distinct landmark volume, not final tree art. A northwest crypt
has a reachable side nook and a worn trail around its back, returning to the
tree approach. The maintenance house sits outside the east cemetery boundary;
its south face addresses a shallow bank, with a ramp at each end of the service
path. Neither loop requires a new climb, drop, darkness or visibility rule.

Future terrain and props should follow use and drainage:

- Cluster trunks and roots along the shaped forest edges, with taller/darker
  masses behind the clear route. Keep branch silhouettes off the central path.
- Use settled gravel on the public cemetery walk, bare earth along the outer
  service loop and softer leaf litter against walls and root bases. Wear joins
  the gates, house frontage and crypt trail; avoid uniform scattered stones.
- Graves and low boundaries form coherent burial rows behind the public walk.
  Leave the central clearing and opposed gates open for bodies and warning cues.
- Keep tools, stacked stone and a small work surface beside the maintenance
  house, behind its access face. The two ramps remain visibly clear.
- Concentrate broken masonry near the crypt and the broken boundary. A worn
  path provides a readable secret cue without hiding it behind darkness.
- Lanterns belong at gates and turns, with restrained pool sizes. No fog or
  lighting setting may change simulation visibility or hide actionable cues.
